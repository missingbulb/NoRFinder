Privacy policy

Last updated 10 November 2026

rauntruth.com is a free research tool for viewing and analysing microscopy images, run by Ariel (ariel@rauntruth.com). It runs in your browser. The site has no server of its own: no account database and no store of anything about you. This policy says what stays on your device, and what reaches other services, and when.

Your images and results

Images are analysed on your own computer, inside your browser. They are never uploaded to us. The site sends them to no one else either, with the exceptions a person asks for: a study's data manager can have it save small previews of a study folder's images into a Previews folder in that study's Google Drive root, and the images they edit back into the study folder they came from. The results stay in your browser unless you download them yourself or submit ground truth, which the site saves into the study's Google Drive folders (below).

What stays on your device

To start faster, to work without the internet and to remember your choices, the site keeps these in your browser's storage on this device:

  • a copy of the site's own pages and code, so it opens without the internet;
  • Python and its packages, so the page starts without downloading them again, including those of the studies you make available offline;
  • images you opened from Google Drive (unless you turn off Auto-save images in Settings), the ones you choose in you keep when making a study available offline, small pictures of them, and Drive folders' preview files;
  • what each Google Drive folder you open in the Drive dialog holds (its files' names, sizes and dates), so the dialog opens without the internet;
  • in Chrome and Edge, for each file you open from your computer in the Image Viewer, its name and the browser's reference to it, so the recent list opens it again, never a copy of the file;
  • your settings, such as the theme, the tips you closed and your last Google Drive links;
  • your work on each image, such as your selections and corrections;
  • your last 200 actions on the page, such as the buttons you clicked, and the errors it met, never what you type or an image's name, so that Report a problem can list the last few in the GitHub issue it opens for you to read and submit;
  • when you are signed in, your name, email address and picture, and Google's sign-in token for about an hour (see below).

The avatar menu's Manage storage lists all of it, with sizes, and deletes any of it. None of it is sent to us.

Services the page reaches

  • Cloudflare serves the site's files, and counts visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics. It sets no cookies, stores nothing on your device, and doesn't follow you across sites. It receives the page's address, the page that linked to it and your browser's details, never your images or results. See Cloudflare's privacy policy.
  • A study's ground-truth picture: while you tag ground truth in a study that names one, your browser loads that picture from the site it lives on (in NoR Finder, urbananimal.co.in), which receives your browser's details and this page's address, never your images or results.
  • jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net) delivers Python and its packages to your browser on the first visit, then again every 30 days, and, in a study that reads printed text, the engines and models that find and read it and the word list it spells what it reads by, when it first needs them; and when you make a study available offline, which also asks it how large each package is. See jsDelivr's privacy policy.
  • Google answers when you open a Google Drive link you pasted (through the Drive API, as you while you are signed in), when making a study available offline lists, or downloads, the images in its Drive folder, when you sign in with Google, and, while you are signed in, when the page reads or saves your settings file (see below); for a study's data managers and Ground Truth Taggers, when the page lists, checks and adds files in that study's Drive folders (see below); and for anyone, when the page reads which images have ground truth, and an image's curated ground truth, from the study's ground-truth folders; Google's picker, which asks which of those folders the site may use, loads from apis.google.com. Your profile picture and Drive's small pictures of images are loaded from Google's image servers. See Google's privacy policy.
  • GitHub is reached only when you click a link to it, for example to report a problem or propose a study. What you post there is public, under GitHub's privacy statement.

Signing in with Google

Signing in is optional; everything else works without it. When you sign in, Google asks your permission for the site to:

  • see your name, email address and profile picture, to show who is signed in in the avatar menu;
  • see, create and change its own settings file in a hidden folder in your Google Drive (the app data folder), to keep your settings the same on every device you sign in on;
  • see and download the files in your Google Drive, so that while you are signed in the Google Drive dialog opens the images and folders your account can see, as you, whether or not they are shared publicly. The site reads the folders and images you open in it and, for a study you open, its root folder's list, the settings file in it and whether you may add files to that root and to its ground-truth submissions folder, to offer you the study's roles. It never changes anything with this permission.

Google has not yet verified the site for reading your Drive, so its sign-in window warns that the app is unverified; continuing past the warning signs you in as above.

If Google Drive lets you add files to a study's ground-truth submissions folder, you may work as its Ground Truth Tagger; if it lets you add files to the study's root folder, as its data manager. Choosing to approve its folders asks Google for its per-file Drive permission, and then shows Google's picker with just that study's folders, for you to choose the ones the site may use. In the folders you choose, the site adds files for you: a tagger's ground-truth file; a data manager's preview files, in a Previews folder it makes in the root, markers saying an image needs ground truth, in the curated ground-truth folder, the images they edit, in a folder named after the image, the ground truth they accept, and, when they set up the study's ground truth, its ground-truth folders and a settings file naming them in the root, the curated folder then shared with anyone with its link to view. It changes, marks or moves to the bin only files the site itself added, for you or for another of the study's taggers and data managers: a data manager accepting or rejecting your ground truth copies or marks your file. It never changes, moves or deletes a file someone made outside the site.

The site changes nothing in your Google Drive but its own settings file and, in the study folders you chose, the files it adds there for you. It reads the folders and images you open in its Google Drive dialog, and the study folders it shows you. The settings file holds only the settings listed in the avatar menu's Settings.

How this data is handled:

  • Accessed and used: your name, email address and picture are shown in the avatar menu. Your settings file is read when you sign in and on later visits, and replaced when you change a setting. When you submit ground truth, your email address names your file and is written into it. In the study folders you chose, the site reads which ones you may add files to, and adds the files you ask it to. A folder you open in the Google Drive dialog is listed (its files' names, sizes and dates), and an image you open there is downloaded into your browser, as for a public link. Nothing else is done with any of it.
  • Stored: your name, email address, picture and Google's sign-in token (which expires after about an hour) are kept in your browser on this device until you sign out, and so is which study folders Google last said you may add files to, so your roles show while the token is renewed. The settings file stays in your own Google Drive, and the files the site adds stay in the study's folders. We keep no copy of any of it, anywhere.
  • Shared: your email address is in the ground-truth files you submit, which anyone with the study's folder links can read. Otherwise Google user data goes only between your browser and Google, never to us or to any third party. It is not used for advertising, sold, or used to train any model.

rauntruth.com's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

To remove it: Sign out in the avatar menu forgets your details and the token on this device. To delete the settings file, open Google Drive's Settings, then Manage apps, find this site's app (named as on Google's sign-in window) and choose Delete hidden app data. To withdraw the site's access entirely, remove it at your Google Account's third-party connections.

Ground truth

Submit saves your ground truth into the study's submissions folder in Google Drive, as a file named with your email address that holds your marks, your email address and when you made it. A study's data manager may copy it into the study's curated folder, which the study's algorithms are scored on. Setting up the study's ground truth shares the curated folder with anyone with its link, and the submissions folder is shared as the study's root is; anyone with their links can read the files, and Google Drive shows each file's owner, your name and email address, to anyone who can see it. Your file stays there under your Google account, and Drive keeps its earlier versions when you submit again. To remove it, delete it in Google Drive or ask the study's owner.
